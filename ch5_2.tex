\section{Shrinkable and Normal Spaces} \label{sec:5-2}

5.2.1. Definition. Let $\mathscr{A}$ and $\mathscr{B}$ be covers of $X$.

$\mathscr{B}$ is a shrinkable of $\mathscr{A}$ if $B_\alpha \subset A_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$.

$X$ is $\lambda$-shrinkable if every open cover of $X$ has an open shrink.

5.2.2. Lemma. (i) A countably shrinkable space is normal and countable paracompact.

(ii) For any $X$ the following are equivalent.

($\alpha$) $X$ is $\lambda$-shrinkable.

(b) Every open $\lambda$-cover of $X$ has a closed shrink.

(c) Every open $\lambda$-cover of $X$ has a functionally open shrink.

*Proof.* (i) Suppose $X$ is countably shrinkable. Then $X$ is normal. By Theorem 3.3.2 $X$ is countably paracompact.

(ii) ($\alpha$) $\implies$ (b) and (c).

(b) $\implies$ (c)

Assume (b). It is easy to show that $X$ is normal. Let $\mathscr{A} = \{A_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in \Lambda}$ be an open cover of $X$, and let $\{F_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in \Lambda}$ be a closed cover of $X$ such that $F_\alpha \subset A_\alpha$ for each $\alpha \in \Lambda$. Let $H_\alpha$ be a functionally open set such that $F_\alpha \subset H_\alpha \subset A_\alpha$. Then $\mathscr{H} = \{H_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in \Lambda}$ be a functionally open shrink of $\mathscr{A}$.
